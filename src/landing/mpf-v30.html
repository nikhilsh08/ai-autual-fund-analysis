What you learn

Judging an investment comes down to four checks.

Before you put money into a fund, these are the four things to find out.

  1. 1The fund's typeIts category, such as large cap or debt, and what the portfolio holds.
  2. 2The full costThe yearly fee (expense ratio), the exit load and tax.
  3. 3The worst fall, and the return against the benchmarkThe benchmark is the index a fund is measured against.
  4. 4How much of your money to investThis depends on your income, expenses, loan instalments and goals.

The first three are about the fund. The fourth is about you. You learn all four.

The free tools give you the numbers. Understanding them means reading the fund's factsheet and reports, which takes time. This is where AI helps, and where it can mislead you.

Why AI

Intelligence is for sale. Judgement is not.

An AI model's answers now cost little or nothing. Knowing which answer to trust still has to be learned.

What AI allows

An AI model such as ChatGPT can read a fund's factsheet, or pages from a company's annual report, in minutes.

The catch

A model tends to agree with whoever asks, and it can get figures wrong. So you learn to ask fair questions, and to check the figures your decision rests on.

So the workshop teaches each check first. Then how to use AI on it, without writing any code.

Source for "tends to agree": Sharma et al., "Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models" (Anthropic, 2023).

This is why it matters who teaches you to check AI's answers.

Your 2-day path

Level 1 · Day 1

How mutual funds work

What is covered

What you own when you buy a mutual fund. How SEBI's fund categories differ, and how to narrow them to the few that fit a goal. A fixed deposit compared with a debt fund (a fund that holds bonds).

With AI: a fund's factsheet (its monthly summary) explained in plain words.

You walk away with a one-page map of fund categories, to mark for your goal.

Level 2 · Day 1

The costs investors often miss

What is covered

Three costs reduce your return: the expense ratio (the yearly fee), exit loads (a charge for selling early) and tax. See what the yearly fee costs you over ten years, and when exit loads and tax apply.

With AI: the ten-year cost of your own SIP, with the source of each figure.

You walk away with a checklist of the costs you pay on each fund.

Level 3 · Day 1

Check a mutual fund in 5 minutes

What is covered

How far a fund fell in past declines (maximum drawdown). Its returns from every start date (rolling returns). Its return against its benchmark.

With AI: a fund's factsheet read in minutes. You ask a leading question (one that hints at the answer), then a fair one, and compare.

You walk away with a one-page check you can run on a mutual fund in 5 minutes.

Level 4 · Day 2

Overlap and your asset allocation

What is covered

How many stocks two funds share (overlap). How to split your money across equity, debt, gold and cash. You start from your income, expenses, EMIs (loan instalments) and goals.

With AI: you choose your rules from examples (such as how much to keep in cash) and give your numbers. The model applies them and shows its working. You check and change it.

You walk away with a worksheet that applies your own rules to your own numbers.

Level 5 · Day 2

Stocks: the basics

What is covered

How an analyst studies a company: revenue, profit margins, debt, cash flow and valuation (the price paid for each rupee of profit). How to judge a stock idea before acting on it.

With AI: a company's annual report on one page, with the reasons against buying listed first.

You walk away with a fixed list of steps for studying a company.

Level 6 · Day 2

When the market falls, and the basics of gold, deposits and insurance

What is covered

What happened in past declines such as March 2020, and what to decide before the next one. The basics of gold, fixed deposits, term insurance (life cover for a fixed number of years) and health insurance. How to tell insurance from an investment sold as insurance. How to estimate how long retirement money can last.

With AI: your plan checked against a past decline such as March 2020.

You walk away with a one-page plan to fill in for a 30% market fall.
