Why returns alone are not enough

Two funds can have the same annualised return and very different maximum drawdowns.

Many fund lists rank funds only by their past returns. The example below shows what such a list leaves out.

Fund AFund B
Annualised return over 10 years13%13%
Maximum drawdown (largest fall from a previous high)−28%−41%
Value of ₹1,00,000 at the lowest point
₹72,000
₹59,000
Time taken to return to the previous high7 months17 months

Illustrative example with rounded numbers. Fund A and Fund B are not real funds. In the workshop, you see the same comparison on real funds.

A returns-only list shows the same 13% for both funds. The list does not show that Fund B fell 41% from its previous high, or that Fund B took 17 months to return to that high, 10 months longer than Fund A.

A second check compares a fund's return with its benchmark index. Over the 10 years to December 2025, 76% of actively managed large-cap funds in India had a lower return than their benchmark index, the S&P India LargeMidCap.

Source: S&P Dow Jones Indices, SPIVA India Scorecard, Year-End 2025.

Who teaches you

Taught by a former Goldman Sachs risk analyst, explained step by step.

At Goldman Sachs, I worked as a risk analyst in the asset management division. My job was to stress-test funds: to find the market conditions in which each fund could lose the most money. Over my time there, I analysed the risk of $65 billion of mutual fund and hedge-fund assets.

Part of that job was explaining risk to colleagues who were not finance specialists, in language they could act on. In this workshop, I do the same for investors in India who want to make their own investment decisions.

Many fund comparisons online rank funds by past returns alone. In this workshop, you learn the checks a risk analyst runs first: how far a fund can fall, what the fund costs, and whether the fund holds the same stocks as your other funds.

I manage my own money with these checks. I built a research system, using AI to write the code, that calculates each figure from the portfolios fund houses publish every month, and I verify each figure before I use it.

Who this workshop is for, and who it is not for.

The workshop teaches a method for evaluating funds. It suits some investors more than others.

For
✓

Investors who have started a SIP, or plan to, and want to know what to check before choosing a fund.

✓

Investors who manage their own money and want to judge a fund by its risk, not only by its star rating.

✓

Mutual fund distributors who want data to support each fund they recommend.

✓

Students and early-career professionals who want to learn how funds are analysed in practice.

Not for
✕

Anyone looking for stock tips or a list of funds to buy.

✕

Anyone looking for a promise of returns.

✕

Anyone who wants someone else to make their investment decisions.

You make your own investment decisions. The workshop teaches you how to check the information first.

Frequently asked questions

Is this investment advice? Will you tell me what to buy?

No. This is an educational workshop. You learn how to evaluate a fund, but no fund is recommended. For personal investment advice, please consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

I'm a complete beginner. Will I keep up?

Yes. The workshop starts with how a mutual fund works and builds from there, step by step, in simple language, using real funds. Every term is explained when it is first used.

I already invest. Will this be too basic?

Probably not. Many investors have not checked their funds' maximum drawdown, risk-adjusted return, or the changes the manager made to the portfolio during a market decline. On Sunday, these checks are run live on a sample portfolio.

Will you check my own portfolio?

No. The checks are shown on a sample portfolio built from real funds. You can run the same checks on your own funds using the free tools in guide 4. No fund is recommended to buy, sell or hold.

Do I need to attend both days?

Yes. Saturday covers how funds work, what they cost and how to measure risk. On Sunday, those checks are run live on a sample portfolio, followed by asset allocation, market declines and retirement withdrawals. Each day builds on the one before.

When do I get the five guides?

They are shared as PDFs during the live workshop. They are yours to keep.

Is it live, or a recording?

Live. You can ask questions in the chat at any time, and they are answered during the session.

Do I need existing investments or any software?

No. You need a stable internet connection and a computer, tablet or phone with audio. Your personal join link is shown on the confirmation page after payment and is sent to your email and WhatsApp.

I'm a mutual fund distributor. Is this relevant to me?

Yes. You learn to analyse a fund and its manager using data, so you can support each recommendation with evidence. You also receive a certificate of completion.

Will I get a certificate?

Yes. Every attendee receives a certificate of completion from CashFlowCrew.

What if I miss a day, or it does not help me?

If you miss a day because of an emergency, write to support@cashflowcrew.in and the team will help you. If the workshop does not help you, you can ask for a full refund within 7 days of purchase, as per our Refund Policy.
